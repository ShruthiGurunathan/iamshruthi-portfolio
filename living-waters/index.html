01 the experience

Seven ground pads turn individual actions into a shared response through water, light and sound.

An invitation to pause by the water

Site observations showed a contrast between the busy indoor circulation and the more open waterfront. We proposed a place to pause and play, with clear paths so people could still move through the area. This shaped four design criteria from the journal: flow and pause, clear wayfinding, accessible participation and comfort.

Design Process Journal · How site observations and testing changed the invitation to participate

Watch

Gentle water, light and sound run in passive mode so visitors can pause and observe.

Explore

Step onto a pad to trigger a response. In the proposed phone experience, scan before stepping to reveal AR fish.

Join

More visitors can activate other pads to combine effects. Activating all seven reveals the full sequence.

02 prototype testing

Four participants attempted five tasks in a formative lab test using think-aloud observation, task-ease ratings, SUS and a follow-up interview.

Prototype setup · A reduced arrangement represented the proposed seven interaction spots.

Feedback test · Lighting showed when a pad registered someone stepping onto it.

70 / 100 average SUS

Three participants had recorded SUS scores. Joining others received task-ease ratings of 5–6 out of 7. The small lab sample, incomplete records and an activation fault limit what we can infer about the waterfront installation.

Starting was unclear

One participant recognised the pads, while another hesitated. One rated pad activation 3 out of 7 for ease. The design needed a visible cue connecting where to stand with what would happen.

Shared play held interest

Participants enjoyed the lighting and combined effects, although individual sounds and their meaning were harder to distinguish.

03 design refinements

We kept the choice to watch, try a pad alone or join a group. The proposed changes focused on making each choice easier to understand and access.

Clearer entry cues

Use footprints, illuminated pad edges and a short board sequence so visitors can see where to begin and what response to expect.

Distinct responses

Give each pad a recognisable visual and sound response so visitors can identify their contribution when several pads are active.

Accessible participation

Use flush pads, clear routes and activation that can register wheelchair users. Test these in a site pilot.

Cultural interpretation

Develop sound and interpretation with local Aboriginal artists and knowledge holders, including attribution and permissions.

Next step · A staged waterfront pilot

Check sensor reliability, dry routes and drainage, and whether visitors can hear and distinguish the responses in market noise. Test the revised invitation with new visitors and assess the optional phone controls and AR separately.
