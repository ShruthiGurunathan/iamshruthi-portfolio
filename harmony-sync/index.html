05 Harmony Sync

A shared karaoke tablet that helps groups set the room vibe and see who’s up next.

about

Harmony Sync is a family-friendly private karaoke concept designed across a phone, room tablet and shared display. I designed the tablet experience in our team project, from wireframes and room controls to the final prototype and heuristic evaluation.

01 userflows

The tablet guides the group from joining the room to handing the mic to the next singer. A QR code or manual table code connects the devices, and the queue and room status update across them.

How the devices work together

Guests browse and queue songs on their phones. The shared display makes lyrics and the next singer visible to the room, while the tablet handles room settings, performer readiness and audience reactions. After a song, the tablet can show either celebration or encouragement before handing over.

02 wireframes

The tablet went through four Figma iterations. Feedback highlighted unclear onboarding, too many similar settings icons and uncertainty about who would sing next.

Make the handover clearer

Five participants reviewed the tablet against Nielsen’s usability heuristics. The review found six severity-1 issues and four severity-2 issues, including unclear queue position, performer readiness and onboarding. I added a position-in-queue panel, an “I’m Ready” action and a short guide explaining the role of each device.

Separate the main controls

Design feedback prompted a visible manual table-code field, a consistent Home button and fewer settings icons. I also moved the key control closer to its label and made song and singer information easier to distinguish.

What the system carries across screens

Vibe cards, countdowns and labelled audio controls give each stage a recognisable structure. Clearer primary and secondary actions help people distinguish the next step from optional controls.
