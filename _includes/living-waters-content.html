01the experience

A proposed interactive fountain for the Sydney Fish Market waterfront at Blackwattle Bay. Seven flush pads around a central wet zone trigger water, light and sound.

watch

In passive mode the fountain gives people a place to pause, with gentle water, light and sound.

explore

Stepping or wheeling onto a marked pad adds a distinct response. QR codes offer optional AR, while the physical interaction works without a phone.

join

As others occupy more pads, their individual responses combine. Each person can contribute a layer to the shared experience.

02prototype testing

We built a floor-pad prototype to see whether visitors could figure out where to start and recognise their own contribution when others joined. Four people tried it in a simulated market setting.

what the test revealed

The shared light effects drew people in, but starting was harder than joining. One participant rated initial pad activation 3 out of 7 for ease, while joining others scored 5–6. People also struggled to tell which sounds they had triggered. That led us to make the pads easier to spot and give each one a distinct response.

03design refinements

make the first step clear

Visitors hesitated at the pads → add footprints, lit edges and a short guide.

make each response distinct

Individual effects were hard to distinguish → give each pad its own water, light and sound response.

keep access flexible

Visitors wanted a choice in how to join → keep paths clear and propose flush pads and optional phone controls.
